# FR 000644 — AI Stats: show 24-hour and workday-normalized AI Hourly Rate on the same card

**Owner-approved Ready Feature Request, 2026-11-09.** Permanent identity **000644**, temporary Ready priority **000810**, after the currently Ready Mini Watch bugfix FR000643 (000800). **FR-only publication now:** create this one backlog document and advance `development/backlog/identity-state.json` from highWatermark **643 → 644**. ## Owner request and intended experience

**Cost Guard Web → AI Stats** already has the right personality: ten playful emoji cards, concise amusing title tooltips and trait lines. Preserve that design and tone. The **first Overview card, `AI Hourly Rate`**, should display **two clearly labeled per-hour CCost values**, using the **same selected-period Total AI Cost numerator**:

1. **24h rate** — selected-period AI reference CCost spread over **all calendar hours**, treating every date as **24 hours**.
2. **Workday rate** — that same selected-period AI reference CCost spread over **eight working hours per eligible workday**, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and applicable **public holidays** according to Cost Guard's configured workday calendar.

Example of the desired compact arrangement, with illustrative **840 reference CCost Credits** in a 7-day period containing five eligible workdays:

```text
💸 AI Hourly Rate
24h          5 CCost/h
Workday     21 CCost/h
Standard-issue silicon. Surprisingly functional.
```

With Web CCost set to USD, the **same raw numbers** become `$0.05/h` and `$0.21/h` (100 reference Credits = $1.00). Layout may use the existing primary value plus a small secondary line; exact typography/spacing is an implementation choice. **Still one existing card, not a new/eleventh card.** The trait and title help should remain short, lighthearted and useful; hovering/focusing/tapping the title or a small intrinsic label should explain the two denominators. No new permanent status row or technical disclaimer.

## Important verified clarification: current AI Hourly Rate is NOT 24-hour-normalized

In current `src/analysis/ai_stats.py::_cost_metrics()`, **`rate = total * HOUR_MS / duration`**, where `duration` comes from the *union of actual/best-effort AI execution intervals* in `_time_metrics()`. Thus the existing displayed AI Hourly Rate is **CCost per active AI-working hour**, **not** CCost divided by every calendar hour of the chosen period. The owner's request intentionally **redefines this card** to show **24h** and **workday** rates instead of the old execution-hour rate.

The separate **AI Working Time** card must continue to measure the original unioned AI execution intervals. Its data and all other stats, including Total AI Cost, must not change merely because this card chooses different denominators. Do not silently keep the old active-execution denominator under a new `24h` label.

## A. Same numerator, two explicit denominators

1. Use the **exact same selected-period `Total AI Cost` raw Decimal** from the AI Stats best-effort calculation for **both** rates. This is the page's existing fun-oriented CCost calculation, including current supported best-effort cost heuristics where needed; do not refetch, reprice or separately rescan history for the hourly-rate card. All costs incurred during the selected period count in the numerator — **including costs incurred overnight, on weekends and on public holidays**. The workday option changes **only the denominator**, never filters out those costs.
2. **24h denominator = eligible selected calendar-day count × 24 hours.** The goal is a full-day-normalized comparison, not the actual union of AI execution hours, not a provider billing rate, and not time since the user happened to open the page. A currently incomplete calendar day still counts as one 24-hour day for this *day-normalized* display; this avoids a morning/afternoon jump caused solely by a partial current day. Use integer calendar-date boundaries where available, not a per-second rate changing whenever the clock ticks.
3. **Workday denominator = eligible selected workday count × 8 hours.** One eligible day always contributes eight nominal working hours, **not** its actual AI execution/active hours and not whatever hours the user happened to work or use the PC. Eight hours are a reporting assumption; do not infer working hours from prompt timestamps, time of day, source activity or a real attendance record. The calculation is normalized to a conventional workweek, not a claim that the user personally worked those exact hours.
4. Reuse the configured **`timezone`** to decide the relevant local calendar dates and **`workdayCalendar`** for eligibility. The current shared rule in `src/analysis/quota_pace.py::is_workday()` excludes weekends for every calendar and, with `SE`, Swedish public holidays from `src/analysis/timezones.py::swedish_public_holidays()`. Shipped configuration is `Europe/Stockholm` and `workdayCalendar: SE`. Reuse that authoritative logic (or a small common pure helper), not duplicated JavaScript holiday tables or an alternative Christmas/Easter formula. With an empty calendar setting, exclude weekends only, matching the existing configuration contract. Count public holidays that fall on weekdays **once**, without double-subtracting weekends.
5. **The configured holiday calendar excludes public holidays, not personal annual leave, employer-specific bridge days, vacation bookings, or extra company shutdown days.** These cannot be inferred from the user's activity or Christmas-week timing. Do not invent a personal leave calendar or change settings as part of this FR. Existing `SE` holidays (including relevant Christmas public holidays) are within scope.
6. Use unrounded Decimal for both divisions; convert/format to **Credits or USD** only at the presentation boundary, following `src/numbers.py::ccost_amount()` and Web's current selected CCost unit. Standard `/h` suffixes, grouping and two-decimal USD are retained. Changing Credits ↔ USD updates **both** numbers from the same raw values, with no new report acquisition.
7. For **zero AI cost** on an otherwise eligible period show friendly `0 CCost/h` or `$0.00/h` for both defined rates. **Never divide by zero:** if the chosen period contains **zero eligible workdays**, the second line must read something concise such as **`Workday: No workdays`**, not fabricate a zero-dollar rate for nonzero cost. The 24h figure still appears. If both total cost and workday hours are zero, a neutral zero-style Workday value is acceptable under the existing AI Stats fun-first empty-period convention, but must not be presented as a mathematically observed working-hour rate. No `NaN`, `Infinity` or technical unavailable/partial/estimated state.
8. Keep the **hourly-rate** metric identity, card order, icon and ten-stat validation. If extending the server projection with a second raw value and separately prepared formatted unit variants, do so through the existing allowlisted `StatsPeriod`/stat projection and `with_display()` formatting. The browser should only render prepared tokens and help, **not** compute cost, divide dates or convert currencies itself. The existing playful trait may be chosen from the main 24h figure, with sensible thresholds recalibrated for the new meaning to avoid every card permanently falling into the same joke.

## B. Exactly which dates count for each current AI Stats selector

Keep the **five existing selectors, their cost-source windows, current report acquisition/caching and other metrics** unchanged:

- **Today**: the **configured-local current date**, counted as **24 calendar hours** and **8 workday hours if eligible**, otherwise zero workday hours. This remains the current AI Stats Today cost numerator; do not divide merely by the hours elapsed since midnight.
- **7 days**: the existing rolling **7 × 24-hour** AI Stats cost window. For this **normalized display denominator**, use **seven local date slots ending on the current configured-local date**, not eight different dates merely because the rolling instant-to-instant window intersects partial days at both ends: **168 calendar hours** and **8 × number of eligible dates among those seven** workday hours. This local-date convention should be explicit in the tooltip if useful. Do not change the underlying `now − 7 × DAY_MS` membership/cost query to make the denominator easier.
- **30 days**: analogous existing rolling **30 × 24-hour** cost window; denominator is **720 calendar hours** and eight times the number of eligible dates among the **30 configured-local dates ending today**. Do not count an accidental 31st partial boundary date.
- **This month**: from the **first configured-local calendar date of the current month through today inclusive**, not through future dates in the month. For example, on the ninth calendar day it has **9 × 24 nominal calendar hours**, while Workday uses only workdays among those nine dates × 8. No fictitious remaining days of the month in either denominator.
- **All time**: begin on the **earliest supported local calendar date of activity/request evidence included in that All time AI Stats calculation**, and end on the current configured-local date inclusive. Count all intervening calendar dates and eligible workdays, **not merely dates on which a session ran**. Cost-free gaps still contribute time. For a truly empty/unsupported beginning, use deterministic fun-friendly zero behavior, not Unix epoch, installation date guessed from an unrelated file, or an unlimited/zero divisor.

For clarity: the rolling **7/30-day cost numerator** retains its present exact trailing-hour membership, whereas the **new rate denominator is an explicitly labeled, day-normalized calendar view** anchored to the same current local date. This distinction avoids quietly changing all other AI Stats metrics or making a 7-day selection count eight full workdays due to boundary overlap. The goal is comparability and legibility, not a minute-by-minute elapsed-time expense meter.

**DST:** use local *calendar dates* for eligibility, but nominal **24h/date** and **8h/eligible date** in these displayed normalization rates, even on a real 23/25-hour daylight-saving-transition date. Preserve the actual source/timeline timestamps, current period inclusion and all other DST-sensitive metrics. Year/month/holiday rollovers must be deterministic for fixed `now`, zone and calendar, without depending on the browser's system timezone.
